    Scheduling optimizations¶

    The basic scheduler funds every admissible speculative record in a fixed order. Several optional lanes refine that. All but one are advisory: they change when and whether a request is funded, never what it computes, and each is bit-identical to the basic policy when its flag is off. The exception, bound commit, is an algorithmic change with a proof obligation, and it is treated separately.

    Four kinds of speculation¶

    The word appears at four levels. Keep them apart when configuring or measuring.

    Mechanism What runs early What verifies it Switch
    agent branch speculation continuations of unresolved choose, gate, loop decisions the program's own guards SpecConfig.enabled, window, max_spec_inflight
    tool observation prediction the next turn built on a guessed observation the observation policy's byte comparison ToolDef(obs=..., guess=...)
    vLLM token drafting candidate tokens inside one request the engine's token verification EngineSpec(vllm_token_drafter=...)
    bound commit nothing; a losing candidate stops early a monotone score bound plus k strictly better finals choose(bound=...)

    Hints¶

    choose(pool, by=ranking, hint=score_vals) attaches per-member evidence. A hint that resolves after the decision's records registered still lands on the decision and re-kicks the scheduler; because a speculative descendant's guard carries its ancestor's At literal, the freshest score reorders that candidate's whole subtree at any depth. Beam search passes each round's score Vals as hints, so under a window of 2 the grandchildren of the currently best candidates are funded before the children of worse ones. A wrong hint costs capacity, never correctness.

    Loser prediction¶

    choose(..., predict_losers=fn) registers a fast, non-blocking callable that names pool indices currently predicted not to be selected. Their pending decodes are demoted below all speculative work, and their in-flight requests are cancelled only when admissible work wants the slot. A pruned record stays admissible: a ranking that consumes its output still resolves, and a mispredicted member reissues with the same NodeId and seed, so the committed trace is byte-identical to no pruning. Beam search enables this for prm with min aggregation (--prune-margin), where a parent's previous-round aggregate is an advisory upper bound on its children.

    Expected-value gate¶

    SpecConfig(ev_gate=True) replaces blind breadth with a per-record estimate:

    EV(r) = P_commit(r) × T_saved(r) − C_interference(r)
    • P_commit multiplies, over the record's unresolved guard literals, a slot-world selection probability built from the decision's live member hints: a member's current rank among scored siblings, softened toward the pool prior k/P by the unscored fraction, split across its k slot worlds by a kernel around its rank, clamped to at least half a uniform share. Bound-dead members get 0.
    • T_saved sums, per decision site, an EWMA of the observed adjudication latency (from the last direct waiter's data-ready to resolution), minus the time this record has already waited. Until a site has any sample the record is cold and is gated on P alone against ev_gate_p_floor (default 0.05).
    • C_interference is the slope of an online regression of engine step interval against running batch size, times the record's max_tokens.

    Speculative candidates are funded by EV descending and cut at EV ≤ 0. Demanded records are exempt for liveness. Rejected records stay pending and are re-evaluated on the next pass.

    Speculative slot preemption¶

    With β saturated, the basic policy freezes funding order: an early-registered candidate holds its slot even when strictly better-ordered work becomes ready behind it. SpecConfig(spec_preempt=True) lets a data-ready, admissible record blocked only by β or by slots held by other speculation cancel the worst running speculative victim it strictly dominates (shallower depth, or equal depth and strictly higher effective hint). Never demanded, committed, or nearly-done (within 25% of its budget) work. Churn is bounded: one live claim per preemptor, at most one preemption per (preemptor, victim) pair, and the victim is not re-fundable while the claim on its slot lives. The victim returns to pending with its identity intact.

    Prefix prefetch¶

    SpecConfig(judge_prefetch=True) warms consumers' prompt prefixes while their producer streams. When a producer has emitted prefetch_chunk_tokens new tokens, each registered consumer whose spine embeds it gets its already-materialized prefix plus the partial output sent to the consumer's engine as a max_tokens=1 request at the lowest engine priority, output discarded. Same-engine forks are skipped because decoding the parent already cached the blocks. prefetch_on_stream(source, build, model="judge") covers consumers the trace cannot express yet, such as a PRM sweep prompt that is a transformation of the candidate text: build(text_so_far) returns the prompt to warm. Prefetch traffic has no record, no ledger row, and no identity; only counters.

    Static plans¶

    SpecConfig(static_plan=graph.plan()) uses a compiled shape to replace the global window with per-decision depth bounds and to cap the number of distinct pool members whose subtrees are funded concurrently at k. Decisions are matched by call site. A blocked sibling stays pending and funds when a charge releases or its guard commits, so results never change.

    Depth-1 reservation¶

    SpecConfig(depth1_unmetered=True) exempts depth-1 continuations from β. Their count is bounded by the decision's pool, and metering them lets deeper speculation starve a late-finishing candidate's children. β keeps metering depth 2 and beyond.

    Bound commit¶

    Bound commit is not speculation. It is an early-stopping rule that is part of the algorithm and is active with speculation on or off.

    Suppose each candidate i has a declared upper bound b(i) on its final score that also holds for every possible truncation of its unfinished generation. Then the moment at least k rivals have resolved final scores strictly greater than b(i), candidate i provably cannot be in the top k, and its decode is terminated as done with the tokens so far, finish_reason="bound". Ties never cancel. The selection and every survivor's bytes are identical to the non-truncated algorithm on every timing schedule, because a cancelled member sits strictly below k full finals and its truncated score can only occupy ranks the selection never reads. The proof is in scheduler.bound_dead_indices, which both the scheduler and the serial reference apply verbatim.

    Beam search provides the contract with bound_commit=True, valid only with scorer="prm", prm_agg="min", and a step_stop: min aggregation makes a running aggregate monotone, and a semantic step boundary keeps the parent's steps re-splitting identically inside the child's sweep, so the parent's previous-round aggregate bounds the child. selection_bounds(pool, bounds, k) opens the contest before scoring, contest.attach(i, score) adds each final as it is minted, and choose(..., bound=contest) ties it to the decision. A final that exceeds its own declared bound is counted as a contract violation. Enable the flag in both arms of any comparison, and expect truncation points to differ between runs.
